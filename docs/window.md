# The window

- **The Opening.** When the app starts, its window shows the cage of a zeolite's framework, the icon's sodalite cage, turning, with the glyphs of the seven agents the app knows moving about inside it, each bending as it goes. It is drawn as the icon is: in characters on a grid, the cage's in the accent color and heavier the nearer they are, a glyph's lighter in the far half of the cage than in the near one. A glyph there is half the size of its art, a character standing for a cell and the cell under it, so that the seven have room in the cage. It lasts three and a half seconds, its characters coming and going one by one, and then the window shows its page; a click, Escape or Enter ends it sooner, and with Reduce Motion set in this Mac's settings it is not shown. Nothing waits for it: the app looks for its runtime meanwhile.
- **Closing the window stops nothing.** The window goes out of sight and the app runs on, its icon in the Dock: a sandbox runs through the app, which keeps its gate open and carries what it sends, so its containers stay as they are in Docker. A click on the Dock icon shows the window again, its Panes as they were. Quitting the app, with ⌘Q, stops every sandbox.
- **Glyphs, not words.** What a control does and what a sandbox's state is are **glyphs**: small pictures drawn from grids of characters in the source, as the icon is. Rest the pointer on one and it says what it stands for. What takes a paragraph to explain is behind an ⓘ glyph, in the form and elsewhere.
- **Cards.** Each sandbox is a Card in the sidebar: its name and the glyph of its state (a ring: stopped; dots: starting; a disc: gate open; a padlock: gate locked; an exclamation mark: an error). You name a sandbox in its Setup, and no two have one name. Rest the pointer on a Card and it shows the sandbox's facts: its state, the agents that run in it, what it leaves by, the exit address seen and expected, the probe's latency, the packets passed and rejected, its clock and its packs' daemons. A sandbox **needs you** while its gate is locked; while one of its Terminal Sessions raised its hand, rang the bell or sent a notification (OSC 9, OSC 777) and you have not looked at that session since; when an agent ended in a session that was hidden; and when a session ended by itself while another page was shown. Its Card then wears the **Attention mark**, a raised hand, says why among its facts, and comes before the Cards of the sandboxes that do not need you. Looking is what clears it: showing the sandbox's Panes. A locked gate is cleared by the gate.
- **The sidebar** is two lists: the sandboxes' Cards, and under them a row for each [Shared Folder](shared-folders.md), which shows the folder's page and says, under the pointer, which sandboxes are given it. Its edge is dragged to make it wider or narrower: it stays right of the window's controls, which sit over the sidebar, as far from the last of them as the first is from the window's edge, and the sidebar takes a fifth of the window's width at most, in a window made narrower too.
- **The Header**, the bar at the top of a sandbox's page, holds its name, the glyphs of its Agent Sessions, and its controls, each a glyph: MCP Calls, once a call crossed one of its bridges, which lists them in the Panes' place; Start, or Stop; Setup, while it starts or runs, which shows the Setup in the page's place and the page again; Clone and Destroy.
- **No sandbox is bound to an agent.** The app chooses no agent and installs none. A running sandbox gives you login shells; what runs in them is what you type. The one agent the app starts is the one an editor on this Mac names to a sandbox's [ACP Bridge](acp-bridge.md), where you gave the sandbox one. The agents the app knows, Claude Code, Codex, Grok, OpenCode, Kimi Code, Pi (oh-my-pi) and Antigravity, it knows by the name each one's own documentation starts it by, and nothing of them is in a sandbox's configuration.
- **Viewports.** A pack that has a display, as a browser's, is shown in a Pane, in the place of the Pane's Terminal Session: each Pane's strip has a control for it, a globe, and several Panes show it at once. The session that gives way is Hidden if it is an Agent Session and ends if it is a plain shell's, which the control says before you click it. New Session, a prompt in the same strip, gives the Pane a login shell again. That, like Close Pane, closes only what the Pane showed: the page stays open in the browser, and the next Pane that shows the browser takes it up.
  - **The control is dimmed**, and says why under the pointer, while the browser is not ready; while the sandbox has 85 hundredths of its memory in use or more; and on a Pane smaller than 480 by 340. The memory is the whole workstation's, as the kernel accounts for the container, less what it holds of files and gives back when asked; where the workstation cannot read it, nothing is dimmed for it.
  - **A Pane that shows a browser keeps 480 by 340.** A divider dragged against it stops there, the terminals beside it giving way first, and so does the sidebar's. Add Pane Beside and Add Pane Below are dimmed where the page has no room left for another Pane. The window itself is not made smaller than its Panes need, and is 900 by 600 at the least again once the page shows something else.
  - **Typing.** A click on the frames gives them the keyboard: what you type then is typed in the page, and what an input method composes, as for Chinese, shows in the page while it is composed. The Edit menu's commands are the page's then: Select All, Undo and Redo are done there; Copy and Cut put what is selected in the page on this Mac's clipboard, and Paste types what the clipboard holds. What is selected is the page's to say: a field for a password says nothing, and neither does a frame of another site inside the page.
  - **Browser Tabs.** Each Pane shows a **Browser Tab** of its own, which no other Pane shows: the first that is free, the one last used first, or a blank new one where every one is shown, with the keyboard in the Address Bar. Over the frames stands the **Address Bar**, which holds the address of the Browser Tab shown, and sends that tab to what you type there: `example.com` goes over HTTPS, `localhost:8000` over HTTP. Before it stand **Back** and **Forward**, which send that tab to the address before and after the one it is at in its own history; while the frames have the keyboard, ⌘[ and ⌘], a swipe between pages and a mouse's side buttons do the same. After it stand **New Browser Tab**, which opens a blank one and gives the keyboard to the Address Bar, for where it is to go, and **Close Browser Tab**, which closes the one shown: another that is free is shown, and where there is none a blank one, with the keyboard in the Address Bar. Where the browser has more than one Browser Tab, as when an agent opened another, a strip over the bar names each by its own title, and a click there shows that one; one that another Pane shows changes places with this Pane's.
  - **A Browser Tab behind another.** Every Browser Tab the app opens has a window of its own in the browser, so that it draws while the others do: a browser draws the front tab of each of its windows and no other. A tab an agent opens through the pack's MCP server comes to the front of the browser's first window, before the tab that was in front there, which draws nothing while it is behind. A Pane that shows such a tab keeps the frame it last drew and says "Behind another Browser Tab"; a click on its frames brings the tab forward. The app brings none forward by itself: the window of a tab that was brought forward is where an agent's next tab comes to the front.
  - **A link in a Pane, clicked with ⌘ held**, is never opened by this Mac. A web address, in a sandbox that has a browser, opens in a new Browser Tab of that browser: in the first Pane that shows the browser, or else in a Pane that is added for it beside the one you clicked in, or below it, where a Pane of 480 by 340 has room; the keyboard stays in the terminal. So a link an agent prints to sign in is opened where the agent waits for its answer, and leaves by the sandbox's gate. Anything else you click there, a web address that no Pane has room to show, and every link of a sandbox that has no browser, is copied to this Mac's clipboard, and the Pane's strip says "Copied" for a moment: paste it where you want it opened. A title and an address are the page's own words: they are shown there, and nowhere else in the window.
- **Panes.** A sandbox that starts shows one Pane: a login shell in its workstation, as the agent's user, in `/workspace`. Its prompt begins a line of its own, though the program before it ended without ending its line. Each Pane has a strip with its name and its controls. **Add Pane Beside** gives the right half of the Pane to a new one, with a login shell of its own, and **Add Pane Below** the lower half: any Pane is divided again the same way, until four stand side by side and four one over another, sixteen at most. A control that can add none is dimmed, and says why under the pointer. Each divider is dragged by itself, and a double click on one puts it back in the middle. A Pane is named by where it stands, so the Panes after a new one are named anew. Close Pane ends the Pane's shell and everything started in it, and the Pane that shared its room takes all of it; the last Pane stays. In a narrow Pane the strip's name gives way to its controls. A shell that ends by itself leaves its Pane, which says so and offers a new session there: none is started unasked.
- **Agent Sessions.** A session in which an agent runs is an Agent Session. The workstation's supervisor tells the app which agent it is, from the names of the session's processes and nothing the session printed: a program started under an agent's name (`claude`, `codex`, `grok`, `opencode`, `kimi`, `omp`, `agy`), or a script of that name under `node`, `bun`, `deno` or `python`. A shell script named like an agent is not taken for one. It is read every two seconds.
  - Its agent's **glyph** stands in the Header, in the order the sessions were opened, and its Pane's strip names the agent. The glyph says, under the pointer, where its session shows.
  - **Hide**, in the Pane's strip, takes the session out of its Pane, which gets a login shell of its own. The session goes on, **Hidden**: its glyph is muted. A click on the glyph shows it in a new Pane, where there is room for one: beside the last Pane that can still be divided, or under it where four stand side by side already.
  - **Drag a glyph onto a Pane** to show its session there; the Pane shows a wash of the accent color while the glyph is over it. A session that another Pane shows changes places with what was there. A Hidden one takes the Pane, and what was there loses it.
  - **A session that loses its Pane**, to a session dropped there, to a browser shown there or by Close Pane, is Hidden if it is an Agent Session, and ends if it is a plain shell's: nothing in the Header stands for a plain shell.
  - A Hidden session that rings, or raises its hand, wears the **Attention mark** on its glyph. An agent that ends out of sight keeps its glyph, with the mark, until its session is looked at: what it last said is on that screen. An agent that quits in sight leaves a plain shell, and its glyph goes.
- **Installing an agent** is typing its maker's installer in a Pane. It leaves by the sandbox's gate, as everything in the workstation does, and lands in the sandbox's Home, so it is there at the next start. A workstation's login shell looks in the places installers use under the home (`~/.local/bin`, `~/bin`, `~/.bun/bin`, `~/.opencode/bin`, `~/.grok/bin`, `~/.kimi-code/bin`, and npm's global packages, which are sent to `~/.npm-global`, the root being read-only) and reads the `~/.bashrc` an installer may have written: what you installed is started by its name, with no restart.
- **What a workstation has to work with**: Node.js and Python with its headers, a C and a C++ compiler and make, git and the ssh client, curl, unzip and xz. Its user is not root and its root is read-only, so nothing installs a package of Debian's while the sandbox runs: what a sandbox needs beyond these, a [Capability Pack](capability-packs.md) of yours adds to its image.
- **The Setup** is the page of a sandbox that does not run: its name, its network, its Shared Folder and the rest of its configuration.
  - **Network**: what the sandbox leaves by and what is expected there, the probe, the resolver, whether the gate keeps a Hold, the Host Conditions. **Check** saves the Setup and starts the sandbox with the Setup still shown: the first line of the network then says what the gate does, in the app's words ("Gate open · leaves by en0 · seen at 203.0.113.7", or why it is locked). The Header's Setup then gives the Panes. Where you list the exit addresses expected at a way out, the field offers the ones the sandbox's own gate has seen there since the app was opened, expected or not: Check with any address expected, or with a guess, and take what the gate saw.
  - **Shared Folder**: one choice of the sandbox's [Shared Folder](shared-folders.md): none, for a sandbox that has only its [Home](layout.md#where-the-app-keeps-things); one of the folders there are; or a new one, whose name is asked under the choice. Under a folder stands whether the agent may run this Mac's programs in that folder: its [Host Tools](host-tools.md), with the GPU and this Mac's name where you add them, and its Allowed Applications: the folders of `/Applications` they read and run, each a row that names one. Under that stands the sandbox's [ACP Bridge](acp-bridge.md): whether an editor on this Mac may work with an agent in the sandbox, and, switched on, a glyph for each agent that speaks the protocol, which copies the command the editor starts in that agent's place.

  - **Clock Policy**: the timezone the sandbox's clock reads in, chosen among the tz database's or taken from the exit address. The clock itself is this Mac's.

  **Start** saves the Setup and starts the sandbox, whose page is then its Panes. A sandbox that runs as its Setup has it is not started again by Check; one whose Setup changed since (another Shared Folder, another resolver) is stopped and started, since nothing of a running workstation is changed, and what was in its Panes ends with it.
- **A terminal draws what agents print.** Its font is Menlo, and for the icons agents draw their screens with and no font of a Mac has, the symbols of the [Nerd Fonts](https://www.nerdfonts.com) project, which the app carries (`internal/dashboard/symbolfont/`, with its license). An agent that paints the whole screen in a color of its own gets the room around the grid in that color too.
- **The app reads nothing a Terminal Session prints.** Which agent runs in a session is read in the workstation, from its processes' names. That a session rang, sent a notification or ended, the app learns from the terminal, MyGo's plugin, which parses what the session prints in order to draw it. The app takes no word of a notification, no title and no screen, and what a Card says is the app's own words: an agent cannot write into the sidebar. The one thing it takes of what a session printed is the address of a link you click with ⌘ held, which it hands to the sandbox's browser, or puts on this Mac's clipboard, and shows nowhere in the window. A test holds it.
- **An agent raises its hand** when it cannot go on without you: it calls the tool `raise_hand` of the MCP server `sandbox`, which every sandbox has and every known agent's registry holds. The server finds the Terminal Session its agent runs in, by going up from itself to the session's shell, and the sandbox's Link carries the hand to the app, which marks that session: on its sandbox's Card, and on its glyph where it is Hidden. It carries no words: the agent says what it needs in its session, and the mark tells you to look. An agent's own bell or notification, where it has such a setting, marks its session the same way.
- **An agent is told how to use its tools, and nothing of where it works.** The server `sandbox` says, when an agent's client meets it, what raising a hand is for and when to do it; the server `macos`, which only a sandbox that is given Host Tools has, says how a program is run on a Mac, what it has there and what it lacks, and what the Wall was given. Each is said as the agent uses the tool, in none of the app's own words: not how a hand is shown in the window, not whose Mac it is. Neither describes the sandbox: what it is, what hosts it, what its home and its Shared Folder are, an agent finds out as it would on any machine. Each tool's description says the same as its server, since a client may show its model the one and not the other, and what it says is true of the tool. A call that is refused is told why the same way: what the agent can do about it, in its own words. The app writes no `AGENTS.md` and no `CLAUDE.md` into a Home.
